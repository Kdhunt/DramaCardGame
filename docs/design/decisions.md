## Requirements carried forward from earlier project discussions

The following were recovered through prior-conversation summaries. Their provenance is documented in [project history](../history/project-history.md).

* Scoring should use explicit numbers and card conditions, with no judgment of acting or narrative quality. Optional narration can supply the comedy.
* Drama Points are intended to serve as both score and spendable currency.
* The tone is campy soap opera comedy, humorous, mildly satirical and suggestive, intended for ages 13 and up.
* The desired formats are approximately 40 to 45 minutes and 90 to 120 minutes. These are design targets, not measured duration claims.
* Earlier planning used three social groups: High Society, Middle Management, and Working Class, with an even distribution requested for an earlier 24-character roster.
* Broad iconic venues such as Country Club and Opera House were preferred over excessively specific locations.

The new permission to replace unsuitable mechanics allows revisiting numerical tier scoring, deck composition, and timing. It does not justify ignoring the later explicit main-character and interference requirements.

## Retain and develop

| Element | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One permanent main character per player | Required | Each player's intervention and benefits must connect to their own character. |
| Relationships, secrets, and visible consequences | Promising proposal | They create conditions for later cards and reasons to protect or exploit prior plays. |
| Cards attaching to characters, relationships, and earlier cards | Required direction | The physical or digital chain should express cause and consequence. |
| Several players contributing before a scene resolves | Required direction | The desired experience is a developing exchange with reversals. |
| Outcome-based and character-based rewards | Required direction | Players can have different motives and several may profit from one resolution. |
| Conditional multipliers | Required family of effects | They amplify a specified consequence or reward under a visible condition. |
| Sharing, redirecting, or stealing rewards through explicit cards or agreements | Proposed way to retain varied distributions | Distribution follows an effect or a negotiated contribution rather than universal ranking rules. |

## Superseded or rejected branches

| Earlier idea | Status and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Shared cast replacing each player's main character | Superseded by the user's explicit main-character requirement. Supporting characters could be considered later. |
| Poker framing, hands, antes, unrestricted wagering, or high/low ranking | Rejected as the central model. The user clarified that Munchkin-style interference is the intended comparison. |
| Fixed half-pot awards for Instigator and Spotlight | Superseded. Mathematically coherent in some settings, but rewards only two universal criteria instead of diverse personal stakes. |
| Numerical class disparity as the whole measure of drama | Replace as the universal engine. It measures social range and misses same-class betrayal, exposure, and jealousy. |
| Performance voting or monologue tie breakers for points | Conflicts with the earlier objective-scoring requirement. |
| Automatic payouts merely for joining or repeatedly touching a chain | High-risk proposal. Reward a resolved condition rather than participation alone. |
| Unlimited multiplication of an already multiplied pool | Reject without explicit bounds. It can overwhelm the intended game length. |
| Extra resources such as Applause, Strings, and a separate Heat currency | Earlier assistant branch, not adopted as the current core. The user also rejected the term Heat. |

## Decisions still open

1. The exact number of contributions and response opportunities per scene.
2. Whether every scene has two outcomes, or some scene cards define additional choices.
3. How main-character traits affect eligibility, influence, costs, and rewards.
4. The relationship between scene Drama, base rewards, and card-specific bonuses.
5. Which rewards may be negotiated and how an agreement is recorded.
6. The cap and stacking rules for multipliers.
7. Persistent condition limits and how a resolved secret can create a new storyline without paying twice for the same fact.
8. Episode count or ending trigger, and actual duration at three and six players.
9. How to prevent early wealth from buying overwhelming future influence.

No assistant proposal in these documents should silently become a settled user decision.
